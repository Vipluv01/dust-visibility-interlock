--- Why the particulate channel carries the opposite sign ---

           day type  dry road %  wet road %  raining %  fog %  loss of control %
PM10 exceedance day     85.8491      9.2453     3.0189 1.3208            13.7736
     all other days     73.3955     23.5439    10.4030 0.4200            17.1992

  daily PM10 against wet-surface share : r = -0.2806
  daily PM10 against fog share         : r = 0.1073
  share of exceedance collisions in March: 48.6792%

  A regional PM10 exceedance in Great Britain is predominantly a dry,
  settled, anticyclonic day, and the March concentration is consistent with
  spring Saharan dust intrusion. Loss of control needs a wet or icy surface.
  So PM10 exceedance is negatively associated with loss of control through
  the weather regime, not because airborne particulate is protective.
  PM10 does track fog (fog is 3.1448x more likely on an
  exceedance day), so the two channels do co-occur - but a station-scale
  air-quality feed cannot stand in for a micro-scale visibility reading.

=== PART 5: THE STAMP SOFTWARE SAFEGUARD ===

--- Hierarchical control structure (three tiers) ---

  TIER 1  Fleet Safety Management
          downward : route and dispatch policy, speed policy, ODD limits
          upward   : incident reports, interlock audit logs, exceedance trends
             |                                    ^
             v                                    |
  TIER 2  Vehicle Visibility Supervisor  (the software controller)
          downward : target-speed command, hazard-light command, advisories
          upward   : visibility and particulate readings, AI severity
                     prediction, driver override state
             |                                    ^
             v                                    |
  TIER 3  Actuators and driver
          downward : engine torque limit, service brake, hazard lamps
          upward   : wheel speed, deceleration achieved, override switch

--- STPA: unsafe control actions for 'Issue Visibility-Loss Speed Restriction' ---

  [Not provided]
    UCA        : Plume entered, no speed restriction issued
    Hazard     : H-1 Goods vehicle strikes a vehicle or obstacle hidden by the plume
    Constraint : SC-1 Any visibility degradation, or any missing visibility reading, must command the visibility-limited speed within one control cycle

  [Provided incorrectly]
    UCA        : Restriction issued, but to a speed the sight distance does not support (the brief's flat 20 mph), or clamped into high-speed flow
    Hazard     : H-1 on a short sight distance; H-2 rear-end collision on a fast road
    Constraint : SC-2 Target speed must be the lesser of the physics limit and the posted limit, never below the road-class floor; hazards precede the slowdown and the deceleration is rate-limited to 2.5 m/s^2

  [Too late]
    UCA        : Restriction issued after the vehicle is already inside the plume
    Hazard     : H-1 The vehicle is travelling faster than its sight distance permits
    Constraint : SC-1 trigger is the leading particulate indicator, not the visibility outcome, so the command precedes the sight-distance collapse

  [Stopped too soon]
    UCA        : Restriction released while the plume still surrounds the vehicle
    Hazard     : H-1 Speed returns before sight distance does
    Constraint : SC-4 Release requires ten consecutive seconds of confirmed-clear readings
